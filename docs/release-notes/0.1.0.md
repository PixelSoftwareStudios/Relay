## Relay 0.1.0, the first release

Relay is voice calling for your local network. No account, no server, no cloud: devices on the same Wi-Fi or LAN find each other and talk, with low latency and encrypted audio. This is the first public release, so everything below is new.

### What you can do

- **Call devices on your network.** Nearby devices appear on their own. You can also connect by a `relay://` address, shown as text and a QR code, and choose whether your device is discoverable or private.
- **Talk your way.** Push-to-talk or open mic, per call, with a global hotkey on the desktop and a talk button on the phone. A call never opens your microphone until you allow it.
- **Decide who can reach you.** Trust, verify, forget or block any device. "Who can ring me" is Anyone, Only members of my channels, or Nobody. Verified contacts can be set to answer automatically, so you can check on a laptop from a distance.
- **Use channels.** Create or join named channels, optionally listed on the network, password-protected or invite-only. Listed channels show up under "Channels on your network" with a Join button, and a password channel asks for its password first.
- **Chat and share files in a call.** Text, images and file offers, with size limits and an accept step.
- **Stay reachable.** On Android a background service keeps your phone ringing while the screen is off. On Windows and macOS, closing the window hides Relay to the tray, and on any desktop you can turn on background availability so the engine rings even without a window open.
- **Hear a clean call.** Opus audio with a jitter buffer and noise suppression on by default at a moderate level.
- **Ring your way.** Built-in or custom ringtone, notifications, and a choice of how an incoming call alerts you.
- **Use it in your language.** English, German, Spanish, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian and Simplified Chinese.
- **Update safely.** The desktop app can check for a new version when it starts (off by default) or when you press the button. An update is downloaded from this repository, verified against a signature that is built into the app, then installed and restarted.
- **Reset when something is wrong.** Settings → Advanced can delete the local database even when the background program will not start. Your device identity is kept.

### Where it runs

| | |
|---|---|
| Desktop app | Windows, macOS 13 and later, and Linux, on x64 and arm64 |
| Command line | `relay` (commands and a full-screen terminal interface) and `relayd` for all of the above |
| Android | arm64 phones, installed by sideloading the APK |

### Privacy and security

- Relay only works on your local network. The engine accepts connections only from devices on the same network as one of your own interfaces, and never from VPN or cellular interfaces unless you turn on an overlay mode.
- Calls are authenticated and encrypted between the two devices. A device's key is pinned the first time you meet it, and a changed key is refused until you confirm it. Compare fingerprints to verify a contact.
- No accounts, no analytics, no telemetry. The only internet request the app can make is the optional update check to GitHub, which you must turn on or start yourself. The Android app contains no Google services.
- The Android APK is signed with the Relay release key. Desktop packages carry a detached signature too (`.minisig`), and every release lists SHA-256 checksums.

### Known limitations

This is an early release. Please read these before relying on it:

- **No echo cancellation yet.** Use headphones, or hold the phone to your ear, to avoid feedback.
- **Desktop packages are not code-signed or notarized.** macOS and Windows will warn the first time you open Relay (instructions below).
- **After a macOS update**, the system may ask once more for permission to use the keychain item that holds your device identity.
- **Real-world testing is still thin.** Calls have been tested between two engines and in emulation, and the Windows, Linux and macOS packages are built and installed by automated checks, but wider testing on real devices is just starting. The first real update will happen with the next release.
- **The channel password protocol has not had an independent security review.**
- **The translations were written by machine and reviewed by a model, not yet by native speakers.** Corrections are welcome. Hindi is marked experimental.
- **Relay is LAN only.** Calling over the internet is not supported.
- **Android builds are arm64 only.**

If you find a problem, please open an issue at https://github.com/PixelSoftwareStudios/Relay/issues.
